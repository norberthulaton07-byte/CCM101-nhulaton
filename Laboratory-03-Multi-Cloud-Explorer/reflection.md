# Mission Reflection

Working through this multi-cloud lab gave me a much clearer picture of what AWS, Azure, and GCP actually offer, and honestly, GCP impressed me the most. I didn't expect a cloud platform's AI and machine learning tools to stand out so much, but seeing how deeply things like Vertex AI and TPUs are built into the platform made it obvious why research and AI-focused companies lean toward it. It wasn't the "biggest" platform I looked at, but it felt the most specialized.

At the same time, researching all three showed me how similar they really are under the hood. Every platform offers the same core building blocks: virtual machines, object storage, identity management, databases, and container orchestration through Kubernetes. The services just have different names and slightly different strengths. That similarity made it easier to compare them side by side instead of feeling like I was learning three completely separate systems.

What stood out most was how much business requirements shape the "right" answer. There isn't one best cloud platform overall, only the best platform for a specific situation. A startup needs flexibility and low upfront cost, a university needs Microsoft integration, and a research company needs computing power for AI. Recommending a platform means understanding the business first, not just the technology.

Acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant for CloudNova Technologies pushed me to think less like a student memorizing services and more like someone solving real problems for real clients. I had to justify my choices, not just list features, which forced me to actually understand why a service fits a use case.

By the end of this mission, my GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio looks a lot stronger. It now has organized documentation, comparison tables, client recommendations, and a decision matrix that shows I can evaluate and explain cloud platforms, not just describe them.
